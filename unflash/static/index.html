Unflash

Detect and repair photosensitive-hazard flashing in videos.

  1. Open a video and Scan it — problem sections are found automatically (WCAG 2.x general-flash and red-flash analysis, plus extended flashes unless the profile is Exact WCAG only). You can also add sections by dragging on the timeline or typing timestamps.
  2. Open a section and Prepare it (proxy + thumbnails + per-frame analysis) — or use prepare all in the sidebar.
  3. Use Suggest (keep-light or keep-dark, optionally restricted to your selection) or mark frames yourself: removed frames are replaced by the last kept frame; extended frames linger for 1 s with silent audio.
  4. Check safety instantly, then Preview render to watch the result. If a check fails, select unsafe frames shows you exactly where the remaining problems are.
  5. Render full-res — this must be done for every section before exporting (render all in the sidebar does them all, skipping up-to-date ones). Sections whose edits changed after rendering are flagged render stale; re-render them.
  6. Export stitches the rendered sections back into the untouched video, then Verify re-scans the final file.

Resuming work. Everything is saved in a <video name>.unflash folder beside the video, and re-opening that video picks it up again — including after the video and its folder have been moved or renamed. If a project isn't picked up automatically (or you moved the folder away from its video), use Open project folder… and point at the .unflash folder itself; the paths stored inside it are repaired, and anything missing is listed at the top of the window.

Extended flashes are 5 s or longer of flashing that meets every WCAG failure criterion except the rate — it runs at the permitted rate (3 flashes/s) instead of above it. WCAG does not fail them, but ITC/Ofcom guidance treats them as a hazard, so the default profile flags them as ordinary work sections (labelled extended flash) that you can edit, check and render like any other. Pick Exact WCAG only if you want them ignored completely. Stricter than WCAG does not flag them either — it already fails outright at the rate the extended test looks for.

This tool reduces risk; it cannot guarantee safety. It does not detect hazardous static patterns (e.g. fine stripes), and individual sensitivity varies — review flagged sections yourself before sharing.
