Unflash

Find the flashing in a video that can trigger photosensitive seizures, and take it out without wrecking the footage.

  1. Open a video, then Scan it. Unflash puts a numbered section around every problem it finds. You can add your own by dragging on the timeline or typing times.
  2. Prepare a section, or use prepare all in the sidebar. This pulls out its frames so you can work on them.
  3. Mark the frames you want gone. A removed frame is replaced by the one before it (R) or the one after it (F), so the picture stays sharp and the timing doesn't move. E holds a frame on screen for a second with the sound muted, for when it has something you need to read.
  4. Or let Suggest do it. Keep-light and keep-dark work out a set of removals and keep going until the section passes. Suggest: reduce FPS is the fallback for flashing neither of them can shift: it thins the section down to a frame rate that can't flash fast enough to fail, going on the frame timings alone and never looking at the pictures. The button says the rate; the ▾ beside it lets you pick your own.
  5. Check safety for an instant verdict, then Preview render to watch the result. If a check fails, select unsafe frames shows you exactly where the problem still is.
  6. Render full-res. Every section needs this before you can export (render all in the sidebar does them all). Edit a section afterwards and it goes render stale, so render it again.
  7. Export stitches the rendered sections back into the rest of the video, and Verify re-scans the finished file.

Coming back later. Everything is saved in a <video name>.unflash folder next to the video, so reopening that video picks the work up again, even if you've moved or renamed both since. If it isn't picked up on its own, use Open project folder… and point at the .unflash folder itself; anything missing gets listed at the top of the window.

Extended flashes are 5 seconds or more of flashing that meets every WCAG failure condition except the rate: it runs at the permitted speed (3 flashes/s) instead of above it. WCAG lets that through, but UK broadcast guidance doesn't and it does affect some viewers, so the default profile flags them as ordinary sections you can edit like any other. Pick Exact WCAG only to ignore them completely. Stricter than WCAG doesn't list them either, because it already fails outright at that speed.

This reduces risk. It can't guarantee safety. It doesn't detect hazardous static patterns such as fine stripes, and individual sensitivity varies, so review the flagged sections yourself before you share anything.
